# Small et al. (2026) — The Illusion of Equality

**APA 7**
Small, P. A., Eshetu, M., Kangriwala, D., Kwafo, H., Lui, J., & Kamelian, A. (2026). The illusion of equality: How meritocracy endorsement reinforces socioeconomic disparities in education. *Social Psychology of Education, 29*(1). https://doi.org/10.1007/s11218-026-10296-w

**類型**：期刊論文（三個實驗＋一個調查）
**全文**：付費牆（Springer）。本檔只存摘要；出版年為線上先行，卷期以出版社頁面為準。
**對陳述文的用處**：把「績優主義信念」本身當成變項——信念越強，越把表現歸因於個人內在特質。你的第 1 節摘要若提到「績優主義的客觀局限」，這一篇給的是心理機制，而非結構機制。

**摘要（原文）**
> Meritocratic ideology is firmly woven into the fabric of American society. Evaluating student achievement under the guise of meritocratic principles reinforces the belief that all students have equal opportunities to succeed regardless of their background. For many, the educational system is not meritocratic. For example, lower socioeconomic status (SES) has been linked to lower academic achievement and delayed academic progress. In the first three studies, we examined how various SES indicators influence attributions for, predictions about, and recommendations regarding student performance. Study 1 found that strong meritocracy endorsers blamed a student's poor performance on internal characteristics such as work ethic and talent regardless of whether the school they attended had ample or deficient resources. In Study 2, participants acknowledged non-meritocratic factors when judging high-performing students, with attributions varying as a function of school resources. Study 3 demonstrated that despite recognizing resource barriers, meritocracy endorsers favored high-SES students in admissions decisions. In Study 4, a brief educational intervention reduced the tendency to rely on meritocratic-based attributions when making judgments about a student's poor performance. These findings suggest that highlighting structural inequities can modify perceptions of student success, potentially mitigating meritocratic biases that disadvantage low-SES students.
